## 2. What Is a Flanking Attack?

In this corpus, a **flanking attack** is a novel real-data analysis executed on the same datasets used in the original ROBUST validation, but asking a fundamentally different question — one that the original framework did not consider and that standard methods would not suggest.

Where a direct attack tests whether the original claim holds, a flanking attack asks: *"Given that the front door is closed, what do the side doors reveal?"*

Flanking attacks were designed according to three principles:

- **Independence:** Each flank uses a different analytical lens (partial correlations, 2D metrics, shape conspiracies, cross-scale coherence, point-by-point local coupling, out-of-sample prediction, morphological decomposition).
- **Falsifiability:** Every flank was pre-specified to have a clear positive or negative outcome — no post-hoc rationalization of results.
- **Cross-domain consistency:** Flanks were designed to test whether patterns found in one domain replicate structurally in other domains.

A flanking attack that **fails** (wrong direction, non-significant, or contradicts RTM) is reported as a negative result and integrated into the paper as a boundary condition of the framework. A flanking attack that **passes** generates a new empirical finding not present in the original document.

## 3. Red Team Audit Standards Met

All **empirical** documents in this corpus were evaluated against the following adversarial criteria:

- **Tautology Detection:** Claims that are algebraically inevitable given the definitions (e.g., α = 2(1 − slope) for flat rotation curves) were identified and removed from the active claim set.
- **Circularity Testing:** Variables used as predictors were tested for independence from the outcome variables using partial correlations, residual analysis, and F-tests. Collinear predictors were flagged regardless of effect size.
- **Out-of-Sample Validation:** Where training and testing were performed on the same data, held-out replication was attempted. Failures were reported with full accuracy metrics.
- **Bootstrap Confirmation:** All surviving partial correlations were subjected to cluster bootstrap (3,000 iterations) to confirm that confidence intervals exclude zero.
- **Effect Size Decomposition:** Cohen's *d* and AUC metrics were computed before and after controlling for confounders. Claims were downgraded when control variables absorbed the signal.
- **Convergent vs. Novel Classification:** Every finding was classified as NOVEL (not predictable from prior literature), CONVERGENT (independently recovers a known result from a different starting point), or CONSISTENT (directionally aligned with known results but not independently derived).
