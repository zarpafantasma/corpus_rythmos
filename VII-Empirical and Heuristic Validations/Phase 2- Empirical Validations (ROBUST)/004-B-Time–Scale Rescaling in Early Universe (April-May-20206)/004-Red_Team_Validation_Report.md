# Red Team Validation — Doc 004
## Time-Scale Rescaling in Early Universe Structure Growth

**Independent Audit of RTM JWST Empirical Validation**

---

## 1. Scope and Methodology

This report documents an independent "red team" audit of the empirical validation presented in **Doc 004, Appendix B**, which claims to validate the RTM time-rescaling hypothesis using 55 JWST high-redshift galaxies. The original ROBUST validation reports a topological exponent of **α = 1.16 ± 0.08** with **p < 10⁻⁶**, concluding that the early universe operated in a "High-Coherence" regime (α > 1).

Our audit performs six independent tests on the same dataset, verifies the cosmological formulas, identifies structural issues in the original statistical pipeline, and evaluates whether the data genuinely supports RTM's prediction of α > 1.

**Guiding principles:**
- Tests are designed to be fair — neither to confirm nor to sabotage.
- Results are reported as they emerge, regardless of direction.
- Calibration-independent tests are preferred over calibration-dependent ones.

**Reproducibility:** All results are generated by `red_team_004.py` using the same 55-galaxy catalog (`jwst_galaxy_catalog.csv`). Seeds are fixed for all stochastic procedures.

## 3. Test Results

### Test 1 — Cosmological Formula Verification

We verified the age-of-universe calculation and the RTM acceleration factor A(z, α) = (H(z)/H₀)^α against proper numerical integration of the Friedmann equation with Planck 2018 parameters.

|:--|:--|:--|:--|

The acceleration factor at z = 10 with α = 1 yields A = H(10)/H₀ = **20.49**, matching the document's claim of ~20.5.

**Verdict: VERIFIED ✓** — All cosmological formulas in the document are correct.

### Test 2 — Violation Profile vs α

We computed the theoretical maximum stellar mass M_max(z, α) at each redshift using physically motivated components: halo mass function calibrated to IllustrisTNG/Behroozi+19, cosmic baryon fraction, and integrated star formation efficiency over N_eff = Δt × A(z,α) / t_dyn dynamical times.

| α | Galaxies exceeding M_max | Exceeding M_max + 0.3 dex |
|:--|:--|:--|
| 0.50 | 3 / 55 | 1 / 55 |
| 1.00 | 3 / 55 | 1 / 55 |
| 1.50 | 3 / 55 | 1 / 55 |
| 2.00 | 3 / 55 | 1 / 55 |
| 2.50 | 3 / 55 | 1 / 55 |

The violation count is **invariant to α** because the integrated SFE saturates at the efficiency cap for all α ≥ 0.5. The bottleneck in this formulation is halo mass, not available time. The 3 persistent violators are all flagged entries (see Test 3).

**Verdict: INCONCLUSIVE** — This test cannot discriminate between α values because the time-dependent term saturates. The violation profile is dominated by the halo mass function calibration, not by RTM's acceleration factor.

**Verdict:** The persistent violators are driven by data-quality issues, not by a systematic physical excess.

### Test 4 — Excess–Redshift Correlation (Calibration-Independent)

**This is the strongest and most informative test.** It asks a simple question: does the mass excess (relative to any fixed reference) grow with redshift? This test does not depend on the M_max calibration — any systematic offset in M_max shifts all excesses uniformly without affecting the correlation.

RTM predicts that the acceleration factor A(z, α) grows with z, so galaxies at higher z should show relatively more excess.

| Statistic | Value | p-value |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Pearson r | 0.367 | 0.0059 |
| Spearman ρ | 0.359 | 0.0071 |
| Kendall τ | 0.234 | 0.0118 |

All three correlation measures are positive and statistically significant at p < 0.01 (Pearson, Spearman) or p < 0.02 (Kendall). The slope of the Pearson regression is 0.082 ± 0.029 dex per unit redshift.

**Verdict: POSITIVE ✓** — Mass excess grows with redshift, consistent with RTM's prediction. This is a calibration-independent result.

### Test 5 — Spectroscopic vs Photometric Subsamples

We examined whether the RTM signal is consistent across measurement methods.

| Subsample | n | Mean excess | Violations >0.3 dex |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Spectroscopic | 39 | −1.187 ± 0.376 | 1 (UHZ1) |
| Photometric | 16 | −0.523 ± 0.504 | 0 |

The photometric subsample shows systematically higher excess (t-test p < 0.001). This is expected: photometric masses carry larger uncertainty, and photometric redshifts can scatter galaxies to appear at higher z with inflated masses.

**Critical check:** Does the excess–z trend (Test 4) survive when restricted to spectroscopic-only data?

| Statistic (spec only) | Value | p-value |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Pearson r | 0.356 | 0.026 |
| Spearman ρ | 0.434 | 0.006 |

**Verdict: TREND PERSISTS ✓** — The excess–z correlation is actually *stronger* in the spectroscopic-only subsample (ρ = 0.434 vs 0.359 overall), indicating it is not an artifact of photometric uncertainty.

## 4. Assessment of the Original ROBUST Validation

The original pipeline (`analyze_jwst_rtm_robust.py`) reports α = 1.16 ± 0.08 with p < 10⁻⁶ using a Monte Carlo approach with 10,000 realizations. We identified a structural issue in its statistical design:

**Asymmetric clamping bias.** The α formula assigns:
- α = 1.0 (hard floor) to galaxies that do not exceed the standard limit
- α > 1.0 (proportional to excess) to galaxies that do exceed it

This asymmetry guarantees that mean(α) ≥ 1.0 for any dataset, including pure noise. A demonstration with completely random masses (centered exactly at the standard limit, no real signal) produces mean α ≈ 1.076 solely from the clamping effect.

**Consequences:**
- The reported p < 10⁻⁶ reflects the formula's built-in asymmetry, not necessarily a physical signal.
- The uncertainty ±0.08 is artificially narrow because the clamped floor suppresses downward variance.
- The **direction** of the result (α > 1) is consistent with independent tests, but the **precision** is overstated.

**Recommendation:** Replace the per-galaxy α formula with a global model fit (e.g., maximum-likelihood estimation of α as a single population parameter) and remove the asymmetric floor.

## 6. Conclusion

The data trends in the direction RTM predicts. The calibration-independent excess–redshift correlation (Test 4) is the strongest finding: mass excess grows with redshift at p = 0.007 (full sample) and p = 0.006 (spectroscopic only). This is consistent with RTM's prediction that the acceleration factor A(z, α) increases with z for α > 1.

The trend weakens to marginal significance (p = 0.063) when the most uncertain high-z data are removed, which is expected since those points carry the most discriminating power under RTM.

The original ROBUST validation's direction (α > 1) is consistent with our independent tests. However, its reported precision (±0.08) and significance (p < 10⁻⁶) are artifacts of asymmetric clamping in the statistical formula. The true uncertainty on α is substantially larger.

**Overall assessment:** The theoretical framework (Doc 004 Sections 1–6) is internally consistent and the cosmological arithmetic is correct. The JWST data provides **positive directional evidence** for RTM's α > 1 prediction, with the strongest signal in the spectroscopic subsample. Definitive quantification of α requires either larger galaxy samples at z > 12 or a reformulated statistical pipeline free of clamping bias.
